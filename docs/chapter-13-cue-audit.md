## Reuse and Limits

Method follows the [broadened review](chapter-1-broadened-cue-audit.md) and
[user calibration](chapter-2-cue-audit.md#subsequent-user-calibration): familiar
categories, actions, ordinary relationships and partial learned name bridges can
be useful without uniquely naming a diagnosis or reproducing every syllable.
Unrelated generic pictures and padded rebuses are not needed to fill a quota.

Worker checked available Chapters1-10 banks, the canonical catalog, and both
sound-bridge workshop records. The Chapter1/5 bleeding-control cue supplies the
red-fluid leak ingredient, but removing its clamp makes hemorrhage a new
composition rather than a whole-cue reuse. Cold/ice, fluid and red-cell tokens are
shared primitives; they are not claimed as novel. The eight whole-cue reuses are
explicitly credited. Chapter7's tranexamic acid hold is not a new hold discovery;
main owns the shared hold register, which this worker did not edit. No held
workshop rebus was resurrected to pad the selection.

Acid/lemon is deliberately a broad name-family token. Miniature plates and
crystals are partial learned bridges, not cell shapes or fluid composition.
The frozen-fluid cue needs taught plasma context; it does not identify a product
from appearance alone. Oxygen debt's wallet may evoke poverty or purchase, and
putty's recoil may be difficult to convey in one static view. Those are untested
design uncertainties, not reasons to impose exact standalone diagnosis recovery.
Shared cold imagery needs context if hypothermia and frozen plasma occur together.
