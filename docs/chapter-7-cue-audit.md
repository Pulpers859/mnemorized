## Review and Limits

Broad method follows [user calibration](chapter-2-cue-audit.md#subsequent-user-calibration).
Familiar objects, spatial relationships and actions count alongside puns.
Not every useful cue needs to uniquely diagnose a disease in isolation.
No render or recall study has tested these designs. No doses, clinical
thresholds, procedures, treatment selection or efficacy claims are admitted.
Pressure-chamber props are mnemonic models, not operational treatment setups.

An independent AI reviewer verified all20 term/page matches and the chapter
boundaries using source text. All retained candidates were considered usable
under the broader learned-cue standard; no empirical effectiveness is claimed.
Main integrated clearer posterior-eye placement for retinal peeling, an intact
eye retaining its displaced lens, an iris/pupil on the globe, skin-like degloving,
floating rather than puncturing pins, and a circulation loop for low volume.
These final refinements are main integration, not independently tested images.

Reviewer suggestions also included more precise shearing motion and showing
oxygen delivery in the chamber. These remain deferred: shears and cylinder/
chamber are retained specifically as word/category cues, not faithful tissue
mechanism or therapy diagrams. Implosion likewise remains a word-direction cue,
not a model of the source's described blast effects. Injury and therapy pressure
chambers must be distinguished by damaged lung versus oxygen token. Recognition
and discrimination still need testing. Earlier banks were checked for reuse by
main; the reviewer did not independently validate those earlier implementations.
This was AI source/concept review, not credentialed clinical review.
